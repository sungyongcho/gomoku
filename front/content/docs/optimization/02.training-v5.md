# A Smaller Network Trained Properly

Meeting a 3-second move target on two Arm Neoverse-N1 cores left no room for the 12-block, 128-channel V4 network, so the optimization work trained a new 6-block, 96-channel network (`v5-small-6x96`) from scratch to iteration 144 (see [Why This Section Exists](/docs/optimization/intro) and [Answering by the Clock](/docs/optimization/time-limited-serving)). Almost every gain in that run came from fixing what went into self-play and training rather than changing the model architecture: cleaned visit targets and tie-breaking, root Dirichlet noise on every searched move, unsearched random openings for both sides, random board translation across empty margins, a temporary share of games against the V4 champion, and in-worker `bfloat16` inference.

Across iterations 1 to 150, training progressed through ten YAML snapshots ([`v5-small-6x96.yaml`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v5-small-6x96.yaml) through [`v5-small-6x96-cont9.yaml`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v5-small-6x96-cont9.yaml)), each triggered by a measurement from self-play diagnostics, evaluation games, or cluster timing. Post-run tournament selection and iteration 144 rankings are documented in [Measuring Strength When the First Player Wins](/docs/optimization/evaluation); weight-decay channel death and pruning are in [What Adam With Weight Decay Did to the Network](/docs/optimization/dead-channels).

## Why a New 6x96 Network Instead of V4

On paid GCP hardware (`c2d-standard-4`), the V4 champion (12 residual blocks, 128 hidden channels in [`deploy.yaml`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/deploy.yaml#L15-L17)) ran 200 MCTS simulations in roughly 2.5 seconds. On two Arm Neoverse-N1 cores of an Oracle Cloud `VM.Standard.A1.Flex` instance, single-sample CPU latency $t_{\text{infer}}$ is much higher, so a large residual tower leaves too few simulations inside the 2.5-second clock limit (`time_limit_s: 2.5`).

Most forward-pass multiply-accumulate work in [`PolicyValueNet`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/model/policy_value_net.py#L68-L149) sits in the $L$ residual blocks, each containing two $3 \times 3$ convolutions of width $C$ over the $19 \times 19$ board:

$$
\text{MACs}_{\text{tower}} \approx L \cdot 2 \cdot 9 \cdot C^2 \cdot 19^2
$$

Halving depth from 12 to 6 blocks and shrinking channel width from 128 to 96 reduces residual-tower compute by $(12 / 6) \cdot (128 / 96)^2 \approx 3.56\times$, leaving only the fixed `13x19x19` stem and compact policy/value heads (`policy_channels = 2`, `value_channels = 1`, `VALUE_HEAD_FC_UNITS = 256`).

On two Neoverse-N1 cores over 2.5 seconds, [`bench_search`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/scripts/bench_search.py) measured ([`deploy/alphazero-oracle/README.md`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/deploy/alphazero-oracle/README.md#L11-L24)):

- `6x96` (`float32` ONNX, 2 threads): 196 searches
- `6x96` (`static int8` ONNX, 2 threads): 634 searches
- `10x128` (`static int8` ONNX, 2 threads): 272 searches

Rather than shrinking V4 or distilling it — a separate distillation script was prototyped and paused, covered in [A Bigger Network and Why It Was Not Deployed](/docs/optimization/bigger-network) — the v5 run initialized a fresh `6x96` network from random weights with no teacher model, no prior replay buffer, and no opening rule restrictions ([`train.py`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/scripts/train.py#L143-L171), [`v5-small-6x96.yaml#L6`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v5-small-6x96.yaml#L6)). Optimization used Adam with `weight_decay: 0.0001` ([`optimizer.py`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/learning/optimizer.py#L24-L27)).

## Training Targets, Loss, and Cleaned Self-Play Data

### Targets and Loss Computation

During self-play, each searched move produces a policy target $\pi$ from root MCTS visit counts `visits_policy / visits_policy.sum()` ([`SearchEngine.get_search_result`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/pvmcts/search/engine.py#L309-L339)) scaled by temperature $\tau$ in [`_apply_temperature`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/agent.py#L19-L53):

$$
\pi(a) = \frac{N(s, a)^{1/\tau}}{\sum_b N(s, b)^{1/\tau}}
$$

In v5, `training.temperature` stayed 1.0, so $\pi(a)$ equals the normalized root visit share. [`sample_action`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/runners/common.py#L21-L48) draws stochastically from $\pi$ for the first `exploration_turns` plies (8 initially, raised to 16 in `cont2`) and plays greedily thereafter. Once a game ends with terminal score $v_{\text{end}} \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$ from the last mover's perspective, [`build_game_record`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/runners/common.py#L62-L123) assigns each stored sample its mover-relative outcome $z \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$:

$$
z_t = \begin{cases}
v_{\text{end}} & \text{if } \text{player}_t = \text{last\_player}, \\
-v_{\text{end}} & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
$$

Each batch (`batch_size: 512`, uniform sampling without prioritized replay in v5) minimizes policy cross-entropy plus value mean-squared error in [`Trainer.train_one_epoch`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/learning/trainer.py#L188-L207):

$$
\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^{B} \left[ -\sum_{a=1}^{361} \pi_{i,a} \log \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(f_\theta^\pi(s_i)\right)_a + \left(f_\theta^v(s_i) - z_i\right)^2 \right]
$$

```python
# alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/learning/trainer.py:188-197
log_probs = F.log_softmax(out_policy, dim=1)
policy_losses = -(policy_targets * log_probs).sum(dim=1)

value_losses = F.mse_loss(
    out_value.squeeze(), value_targets.squeeze(), reduction="none"
)

combined_losses = policy_losses + value_losses
```

L2 regularization (`weight_decay: 0.0001`) is not a term inside `combined_losses`: `torch.optim.Adam` adds $\lambda\theta$ to each gradient before its adaptive scaling. That coupling is what later drove unused channels to zero; see [What Adam With Weight Decay Did to the Network](/docs/optimization/dead-channels).

### What "Cleaned Self-Play Targets" Fixed

Before launching v5, commits [`9f09c80`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/9f09c80) and [`4d61d39`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/4d61d39) corrected four defects that corrupted training targets or rollbacks:

1. **Corner tie-break bias**: After `exploration_turns`, `sample_action` previously picked `int(np.argmax(pi))`, breaking equal root visit counts by lowest flat index `(0, 0)`. [`greedy_action`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/types.py#L19-L24) now selects uniformly among tied maxima ([`runners/common.py#L38-L39`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/runners/common.py#L38-L39)).
2. **One-hot random opening targets**: Random opening plies carry no MCTS visit distribution; storing them as one-hot targets trained the policy head toward uniform random squares. [`SelfPlaySample`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/runners/common.py#L10-L18) added `is_training_target: bool = True`, set to `False` for random opening moves and filtered out in [`build_game_record`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/runners/common.py#L95-L97).
3. **Batched virtual-loss $Q$ formula**: In [`MctsEngine::CalculateUcb`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/cpp/src/MctsEngine.cpp#L817-L820), batched selection previously divided `child->value_sum` by `child_visits` (`visit_count + virtual_loss`) whenever `child_visits > 0` without treating pending virtual losses as losses. Commit [`9f09c80`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/9f09c80) guarded on `child_visit_count > 0` and changed the visited numerator to `-(child->value_sum + child_virtual_loss)`.
4. **Resuming past rejected gates**: [`load_resume_state`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/scripts/pipelines/common.py#L98-L106) ([`4d61d39`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/4d61d39)) loads weights and optimizer state from the latest finished iteration (`iteration_ckpt_path(completed_before)`) while keeping `champion_path` only as the promotion opponent, avoiding silent rollback across stops and restarts.

Commit [`9f09c80`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/9f09c80) also introduced [`replay_report.py`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/scripts/replay_report.py) to measure `early_one_hot`, per-player target entropy, `reply_near`, game-ending types, and raw-policy tactics across shards. That report caught v5's opening shift at iteration 29, when White's first reply naturally moved within two intersections of Black's stone (`reply_near` jumped from `2–4%` before iteration 29 to `53–68%` afterward, noted in [`v5-small-6x96-cont.yaml#L12-L13`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v5-small-6x96-cont.yaml#L12-L13)).

## Root Dirichlet Noise and Unsearched Random Openings

### Root Noise on Every Move and Zero-Prior Children

Without root exploration noise, MCTS cannot revisit moves whose network prior $P(s, a)$ is near zero, even if tactics later change. As documented in [Monte Carlo Tree Search with PUCT](/docs/alphazero/mcts), root priors are mixed with a Dirichlet sample $\eta \sim \operatorname{Dir}(\alpha)$ over legal moves in [`_safe_dirichlet_noise`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/pvmcts/search/engine.py#L15-L58):

$$
P'(s, a) = (1 - \epsilon) \cdot P(s, a) + \epsilon \cdot \eta_a, \qquad \eta \sim \operatorname{Dir}(\alpha)
$$

masked to legal actions and renormalized. Every v5 configuration used `dirichlet_epsilon: 0.25` and `dirichlet_alpha: 0.03` during self-play (0.0 during evaluation and serving).

Two bugs disabled much of that exploration until they were caught in succession:

- **Batched native search dropped root noise** ([`9f09c80`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/9f09c80)): Both `_run_async_mcts` and `_run_sync_batch_mcts` in [`CppSearchStrategy`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/pvmcts/search/sequential/cpp_strategy.py#L146-L215) bypassed root noise until `_with_root_noise` was wired onto the root batch (`h_found == 0` / first sync batch).
- **Ray self-play drew noise on move 0 only, and zero-prior moves had no child node** ([`baefedb`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/baefedb)): In `cont4`, even after games against V4 lifted v5's random-opening score to 0.95, blocking a five along the outer line remained at `0.62–0.63` top-1 accuracy ([`v5-small-6x96-cont5.yaml#L3-L6`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v5-small-6x96-cont5.yaml#L3-L6)). [`VectorizeRunner.play_batch_games`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/runners/vectorize_runner.py#L154-L160) had been passing `add_noise_flags=[(add_noise and slot.turn == 0) ...]`, drawing noise only on the empty first board. Commit [`baefedb`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/baefedb) changed `add_noise_flags` to `bool(add_noise)` so every searched move receives fresh root noise, and updated [`_ensure_root_noise`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/pvmcts/search/engine.py#L293-L305) to create a `TreeNode` for every move in `np.nonzero(noisy_policy)[0]` rather than dropping noise assigned to `prior == 0` actions.

### Unsearched Random Openings for Both Sides

By iteration 50, 96% of self-play games opened on `(5, 2)` and its $D_4$ mirrors, leaving almost no stones 7 or more intersections from the edge; against V4 from random openings, iteration 50 lost 27 of 40 games with a median length of 10 plies ([`v5-small-6x96-cont2.yaml#L3-L5`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v5-small-6x96-cont2.yaml#L3-L5)).

From iteration 52, `cont2` cycled `random_opening_turns` across `1, 2, 3, 4, 0` per iteration (0 keeps empty-board openings in replay) and doubled `exploration_turns` from 8 to 16. Commit [`baefedb`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/baefedb) also fixed how those openings ran in [`VectorizeRunner`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/runners/vectorize_runner.py#L135-L152) and [`SelfPlayRunner`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/runners/selfplay.py#L116-L125): previously, batched workers ran MCTS before overwriting the action with a random square and skipped random openings on opponent turns. After `baefedb`, `opening_actions` are sampled uniformly for turns `< rnd_opening` regardless of which side is to move, excluded from `agent_indices` and `opp_indices` so no search is wasted, and marked `is_training_target = False`.

## Board-Translation Augmentation

Random openings forced stones into new parts of the board on ply 1, yet the network immediately steered play back to its familiar corner: at iteration 56, v5 lost 40 of 40 games to V4 from a center opening and scored an immediate win there at -0.60 ([`v5-small-6x96-cont3.yaml#L3-L5`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v5-small-6x96-cont3.yaml#L3-L5)).

The $D_4$ rotations and reflections already documented in [Training Pipeline](/docs/alphazero/training-pipeline) leave the center intersection fixed and preserve each stone's distance from the center and board edges. Commit [`7860f7c`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/7860f7c) added [`_apply_translation`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/learning/dataset.py#L92-L134), applied right after `_apply_symmetry` inside [`ReplayDataset.__getitem__`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/learning/dataset.py#L171-L174):

1. With probability `translation_prob` (0.5 from `cont3` onward), inspect the union of stones `encoded_state[0] + encoded_state[1]` to find occupied bounding rows $[r_{\min}, r_{\max}]$ and columns $[c_{\min}, c_{\max}]$.
2. Skip empty boards or any position touching the outer line ($r_{\min} = 0$, $c_{\min} = 0$, $r_{\max} = H - 1$, or $c_{\max} = W - 1$).
3. Sample integer shifts (since `np.random.randint`'s upper limit is exclusive, no stone reaches line 0 or $H - 1$):

$$
\Delta y \sim \mathcal{U}\!\left\{1 - r_{\min},\dots,\, (H - 2) - r_{\max}\right\}, \qquad
\Delta x \sim \mathcal{U}\!\left\{1 - c_{\min},\dots,\, (W - 2) - c_{\max}\right\}
$$

4. Shift all feature planes and the 2D policy grid with `np.roll`, build a boolean mask `crossed` for strips that wrapped across a board edge, zero out `target[crossed]` and `_FORBIDDEN_PLANE` (`plane 7`), and renormalize the remaining policy mass:

$$
\pi'(y, x) = \frac{\mathbb{1}_{\neg \text{crossed}}(y, x)\,\pi_{\text{roll}}(y, x)}{\sum_{y', x'} \mathbb{1}_{\neg \text{crossed}}(y', x')\,\pi_{\text{roll}}(y', x')}
$$

```python
# alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/learning/dataset.py:113-133
dy = int(np.random.randint(1 - rows[0], h - 1 - rows[-1]))
dx = int(np.random.randint(1 - cols[0], w - 1 - cols[-1]))
moved = np.roll(encoded_state, (dy, dx), axis=(1, 2))
target = np.roll(policy.reshape(h, w), (dy, dx), axis=(0, 1))
...
target[crossed] = 0.0
moved[_FORBIDDEN_PLANE][crossed] = 0.0

kept = float(target.sum())
if kept <= 0.0:
    return encoded_state, policy
return moved, (target / kept).reshape(-1)
```

Because `translation_prob: 0.5` gates the transform and roughly half of replay states have no stone on the outer line, translation affects about a quarter of all training samples ([`v5-small-6x96-cont3.yaml#L7-L9`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v5-small-6x96-cont3.yaml#L7-L9)). ## Playing a Quarter of Games Against V4

Between iterations 64 and 100, training loss fell steadily from 1.86 to 1.32, yet strength stalled across every benchmark: the latest model scored 49.6% over 480 promotion games against the champion, and the champion scored `0.725–0.875` against V4 on the cluster and `0.75–0.80` against V4 locally from random openings ([`v5-small-6x96-cont4.yaml#L3-L8`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v5-small-6x96-cont4.yaml#L3-L8)). Inspecting 9 local losses showed 8 were five-in-a-row threats along the outer line that v5 left completely unblocked (policy near 0, value +1.0). Because v5's own self-play never constructed those outer-line threats, they made up only 0.2% of the replay buffer.

Commit [`4f30c71`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/4f30c71) added [`ExternalOpponentConfig`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/utils/config/loader.py#L72-L87) inside [`RaySelfPlayWorker`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/runners/workers/ray_worker.py#L298-L352), loading the fixed `v4-champion-222.pt` (`12x128`) weights inside each worker with `dirichlet_epsilon: 0.0` and `num_searches: 400` while v5 kept 800. For 25% of self-play games (`ratio: 0.25` across iterations 101 to 130, alternating Black and White), both sides' searched moves were stored in replay (`is_training_target = True` on all searched plies in [`VectorizeRunner`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/runners/vectorize_runner.py#L223-L231)), feeding v5 both loss outcomes on the value head and V4's outer-line blocks on the policy head.

Playing against V4 lifted v5 from `0.75–0.80` to 0.95 against V4 from random openings in `cont4` ([`v5-small-6x96-cont5.yaml#L3`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v5-small-6x96-cont5.yaml#L3)), and eventually to `39–1` at equal search counts by iteration 130 ([`v5-small-6x96-cont6.yaml#L8-L9`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v5-small-6x96-cont6.yaml#L8-L9)). Once v5 surpassed V4 by `39–1`, keeping `external.ratio: 0.25` meant $0.25 \times 0.5 = 1/8$ of all stored policy targets came from the weaker player, so `cont6` turned the V4 opponent off at iteration 131.

## In-Worker `bfloat16` Inference

Earlier self-play shipped leaf states over Ray from CPU game workers to central GPU inference actors (`use_local_inference: false`). In a pilot run on `2026-11-07` (3 C4D worker nodes plus head), that remote actor path reached about 6,000 positions/s, and its async Ray search also flattened root visit distributions ([`v5-small-6x96.yaml#L4-L5`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v5-small-6x96.yaml#L4-L5)).

Brain Floating Point (`bfloat16`) is a 16-bit floating-point format with 1 sign bit, 8 exponent bits, and 7 mantissa bits (compared to `float32`'s 23 mantissa bits):

$$
x_{\text{bf16}} = (-1)^s \cdot 2^{e - 127} \cdot \left(1 + \sum_{k=1}^{7} m_k 2^{-k}\right)
$$

Because `bfloat16` keeps `float32`'s full 8-bit exponent range ($\approx \pm 3.4 \times 10^{38}$), forward passes need no dynamic loss scaling and never overflow `BatchNorm2d` activations, at roughly $7.8 \times 10^{-3}$ relative rounding precision — more than enough for MCTS leaf policy priors and scalar values.

Commits [`bb34c62`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/bb34c62), [`c146b1f`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/c146b1f), [`47e5258`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/47e5258), and [`b90e04e`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/b90e04e) wired in-process inference via [`LocalInference`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/inference/local.py#L9-L86):

```python
# alphazero/gomoku/inference/local.py:45,80-84
prefers_sync = True
...
with torch.inference_mode():
    if self._bf16:
        with torch.autocast(
            device_type=self._device.type, dtype=torch.bfloat16
        ):
            policy, value = self._model(inputs)
        policy, value = policy.float(), value.float()
```

Three design details mattered in [`RaySelfPlayWorker`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/runners/workers/ray_worker.py#L45-L54) and [`CppSearchStrategy`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/pvmcts/search/sequential/cpp_strategy.py#L88-L94):

- **`prefers_sync = True`**: Forces native C++ search down `_run_sync_batch_mcts` instead of round-tripping tensors through Ray's object store, converting outputs back to `float32` so NumPy and C++ can read them.
- **Single PyTorch thread per CPU worker**: Workers call `torch.set_num_threads(1)` to prevent thread contention across 84 CPU slots, while the head node allocates 13 workers sharing 0.95 of its L4 GPU (`num_actors: 0` in `cont` onward freed the unused inference actor's vCPU).
- **Throughput and denormal immunity**: With local `bfloat16`, the `2026-11-07` pilot jumped from `~6,000` to `~10,700` positions/s (240 games in 15 minutes). Later on the cluster (`v5-small-6x96-cont9.yaml`), self-play `bfloat16` stayed at 5 ms per position on both AMD C4D and Intel C4 nodes, even when ~281,000 weight-decay denormal floats slowed `float32` evaluation on Intel C4 to `206–258 ms` per position (see [What Adam With Weight Decay Did to the Network](/docs/optimization/dead-channels)).

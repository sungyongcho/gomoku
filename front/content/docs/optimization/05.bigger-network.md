# A Bigger Network and Why It Was Not Deployed

After the `6x96` `v5` network plateaued between iterations 116 and 144, a 10-block, 128-channel `ResNet` (`v6` and `v6b`) was seeded on `v5`'s self-play shards and trained further by self-play (commits [`719fe38`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/719fe38), [`79efde7`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/79efde7)). Under equal simulation counts (1,600 searches each), `v6b` defeated `v5` iteration 144 with a score of 0.540, confirming that extra capacity improves each leaf evaluation.

On the serving host, however, answers stop after 2.5 seconds rather than after a fixed simulation count (see [Why This Section Exists](/docs/optimization/intro) and [Answering by the Clock](/docs/optimization/time-limited-serving)). Because `10x128` completes 272 static `int8` searches in 2.5 seconds on two Arm Neoverse-N1 cores against 634 for `6x96`, equal-time matches gave `v6b` only `0.460–0.497` over iterations 16 to 32, a six-setting search sweep left scores at `0.49–0.505`, and a teacher-distillation path (commit [`0039c4d`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/0039c4d)) was paused — so `v5` remained in production while `v6b` was halted at iteration 32 for time and budget.

## Why Try a Bigger Network and What It Costs

By iteration 116 of the `v5` (`6x96`) run, training had stalled: from iteration 116 through 144, neither the 360-game ladder nor the promotion gate could separate checkpoints, and the total training loss stayed near 2.15 ([`v6-10x128.yaml#L2-L4`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v6-10x128.yaml#L2-L4); see [A Smaller Network Trained Properly](/docs/optimization/training-v5)). Spending more self-play iterations on the same `6x96` topology was no longer producing measurable gains, so the remaining training budget shifted to a larger `10x128` network (`v6`).

In a residual network, adding residual blocks $B$ and hidden channels $C$ increases representational capacity at a quadratic compute penalty. As described in [The Policy-Value Network](/docs/alphazero/neural-network), [`PolicyValueNet`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/model/policy_value_net.py#L105-L185) pairs a `3x3` `start_block` (13 input planes to $C$ channels) with $B$ `ResBlock` modules — each containing two `3x3` convolutions of width $C \to C$ — followed by `1x1` policy and value heads (`policy_channels = 2`, `value_channels = 1`, `VALUE_HEAD_FC_UNITS = 256`). Across a $19 \times 19$ board ($H \cdot W = 361$ squares) with kernel size $k = 3$ ($k^2 = 9$), the residual tower's multiply-accumulate operations (MACs) per single-board forward pass are:

$$
\text{MACs}_{\text{tower}} = 2 \cdot k^2 \cdot H \cdot W \cdot B \cdot C^2 = 6{,}498 \cdot B \cdot C^2
$$

Because $t_{\text{tree}}$ for C++ tree operations is tens of microseconds, CPU inference dominates each simulation under `batch_infer_size: 1`, making the 2.5-second search budget scale roughly inversely with $t_{\text{infer}}$:

$$
N_{\text{new}} \approx \frac{T_{\text{budget}}}{t_{\text{tree}} + t_{\text{infer}}}
$$

| Exact weights (`PolicyValueNet`) | 1,364,434 (`1.36M`) | 3,326,354 (`3.33M`) | 2.44x | [`v6-10x128.yaml#L9`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v6-10x128.yaml#L9) |
| Tower scaling factor ($B \cdot C^2$) | 55,296 | 163,840 | 2.96x | [`policy_value_net.py#L118-L185`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/model/policy_value_net.py#L118-L185) |

## Seeding v6 on v5's Games, Then Self-Play (v6 and v6b)

Starting a `3.33M`-weight network from random weights wastes GPU self-play hours rediscovering tactical rules `v5` already knew. Commit [`719fe38`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/719fe38) added a supervised pretraining pipeline so `10x128` could warm-start from `v5`'s replay archive before entering self-play.

### Pretraining and Replay Seeding

[`pretrain.py`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/scripts/pretrain.py#L147-L219) loads `v5` replay shards from iterations 96 through 143, reserves iteration 144 (games played by `v5` iteration 143) as a held-out validation split, trains `10x128` for four passes (`--lr 0.001,0.001,0.0003,0.0001`), and writes `ckpt/seed_v5games.pt`. Because [`ReplayDataset`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/scripts/pretrain.py#L105-L129) applies a random board symmetry per read, `holdout_losses` pins Python, NumPy, and PyTorch RNG seeds before every evaluation pass so `v6`, `v6b`, and the `--baseline` `6x96` model are scored on identical board orientations.

Both pretraining (`pretrain.py`) and subsequent self-play ([`AlphaZeroTrainer`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/learning/trainer.py#L188-L197)) minimize the combined policy cross-entropy against MCTS visit distributions $\pi$ and value squared error against game results $z$:

$$
\mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\sum_{a} \pi_a \log p_{\theta}(a \mid s) + \bigl(\hat{v}_{\theta}(s) - z\bigr)^2
$$

When self-play begins, [`_find_seed_checkpoint`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/scripts/train.py#L77-L84) detects `seed_*.pt` in the checkpoint directory and initializes iteration 0 from those pretrained weights:

```python
# alphazero/gomoku/scripts/train.py:77-84
def _find_seed_checkpoint(fs: fsspec.AbstractFileSystem, paths_cfg) -> str | None:
    ckpt_dir_path = format_path(paths_cfg, paths_cfg.ckpt_dir)
    seed_glob = os.path.join(ckpt_dir_path.rstrip("/"), "seed_*.pt")
    try:
        pretrained_seeds = sorted(fs.glob(seed_glob))
    except FileNotFoundError:
        pretrained_seeds = []
    return pretrained_seeds[-1] if pretrained_seeds else None
```

To avoid a sudden distribution shock at iteration 1, `v5`'s last replay shards were copied into the new run's replay directory as iteration 0 ([`v6-10x128.yaml#L6-L8`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v6-10x128.yaml#L6-L8)), and in `v6b` the games `v6` had already played before restarting were included alongside them in iteration 0 ([`v6b-10x128.yaml#L11-L13`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v6b-10x128.yaml#L11-L13)). Both `v6` and `v6b` kept `v5`'s final self-play settings: 800 MCTS searches per move, Dirichlet noise (`epsilon: 0.25`, `alpha: 0.03`) for 16 exploration turns, 1,200 self-play games per iteration, a 500,000-sample replay buffer (roughly 17 iterations), 1 training epoch per iteration at `batch_size: 512`, `translation_prob: 0.5`, `fpu_from_parent: true` with `fpu_reduction: 0.1`, and `random_opening_turns` cycling 0 through 4.

### Why v6 Was Restarted as v6b

Inspecting the pretrained `v6` seed showed severe filter collapse: trained with `Adam` (`weight_decay: 0.0001`), `v6` lost 497 of 2,691 convolution filters — roughly half of the first convolution layer across residual blocks 2 through 9 — leaving 36% of its weights at zero while still paying full inference latency ([`v6b-10x128.yaml#L2-L6`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v6b-10x128.yaml#L2-L6)). As explained in [What Adam With Weight Decay Did to the Network](/docs/optimization/dead-channels), coupled `Adam` weight decay pushes low-gradient channels to zero at the full learning rate.

[`build_optimizer`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/learning/optimizer.py#L24-L36) added `optimizer: adamw`, restricting decoupled weight decay to convolution and linear weights (`p.ndim > 1`) while leaving `BatchNorm2d` scales and biases unpenalized:

```python
# alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/learning/optimizer.py:24-36
    if training.optimizer == "adam":
        return torch.optim.Adam(
            model.parameters(), lr=learning_rate, weight_decay=training.weight_decay
        )
    decayed = [p for p in model.parameters() if p.requires_grad and p.ndim > 1]
    plain = [p for p in model.parameters() if p.requires_grad and p.ndim <= 1]
    return torch.optim.AdamW(
        [
            {"params": decayed, "weight_decay": training.weight_decay},
            {"params": plain, "weight_decay": 0.0},
        ],
        lr=learning_rate,
    )
```

Restarting from scratch as `v6b` with `optimizer: adamw` and `weight_decay: 0.01` on the exact same `v5` iterations `96–143` pretraining schedule kept every filter alive and lowered held-out iteration-144 loss:

| Run | Optimizer & decay | Filter state after pretraining | Held-out policy loss | Held-out value loss | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `v6` seed | `Adam` (0.0001 coupled) | 497 / 2,691 filters dead (36% zero weights) | 2.054 | 0.319 | [`v6b-10x128.yaml#L2-L10`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v6b-10x128.yaml#L2-L10) |
| `v6b` seed | `AdamW` (0.01 on `ndim > 1`) | All 2,691 filters alive | 2.043 | 0.316 | [`v6b-10x128.yaml#L7-L10`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v6b-10x128.yaml#L7-L10) |

Once `v6b` entered self-play at `lr = 0.0003`, total training loss stalled from iteration 9 through 13 (`2.119, 2.122, 2.122, 2.119, 2.113`). Just as `v5` had resumed improving only after stepping `learning_rate` down from 0.0002 to 0.0001 at iteration 131, [`v6b-10x128-cont1.yaml`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v6b-10x128-cont1.yaml#L1-L7) cut the learning rate six iterations ahead of the original `until: 20` schedule — setting 0.0001 for iterations `15–24` and 0.00005 for `25–30` — before [`v6b-10x128-cont2.yaml`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v6b-10x128-cont2.yaml#L8-L9) extended the target to 50 iterations (0.00005 through 40, 0.00002 through 50).

## Equal Search Counts vs. Equal Thinking Time

Evaluating a heavier network at equal simulation counts answers whether larger weights evaluate a single leaf better, not whether the deployed server will play stronger moves in 2.5 seconds. Because deployment fixes wall-clock time on two Neoverse-N1 cores, only an equal-thinking-time match — giving each network the number of searches it actually completes in 2.5 seconds — decides whether `v6b` should replace `v5` ([`v6-10x128.yaml#L11-L15`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v6-10x128.yaml#L11-L15), [`deploy-v6b.yaml#L1-L7`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/deploy-v6b.yaml#L1-L7)).

Commit [`719fe38`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/719fe38) parameterized asymmetric reference matches in [`run_reference_match`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/eval/reference_match.py#L144-L184). Every match pitted `v6b`'s latest checkpoint (`player: latest`) against `v5` iteration 144 across paired colour-swapped games starting from 3 random opening plies (`opening_plies: 3`, the most balanced opening found in `v5` self-play), with deterministic MCTS (`temperature = 0`, `dirichlet_epsilon = 0`, `batch_infer_size = 1`, `torch.set_flush_denormal(True)`):

```python
# alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/eval/reference_match.py:156-163
    sides = {
        CHAMPION: (model_cfg, match_mcts(mcts, ref_cfg.num_searches), champion_state),
        REFERENCE: (
            reference.model,
            match_mcts(reference.mcts, ref_cfg.reference_searches or ref_cfg.num_searches),
            load_state_dict(ref_cfg.checkpoint),
        ),
    }
```

In [`summarize`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/alphazero/eval/reference_match.py#L122-L141), draws count 0.5 toward match score $S$ over $G$ games; near $S \approx 0.50$, a match's binomial standard error $\sigma_S$ and implied Elo offset $\Delta\text{Elo}$ are:

$$
S = \frac{W + 0.5\,D}{G}, \qquad \sigma_S = \sqrt{\frac{S(1 - S)}{G}} \approx \frac{1}{2\sqrt{G}}, \qquad \Delta\text{Elo} = -400 \log_{10}\!\left(\frac{1}{S} - 1\right)
$$

For `G = 300`, $\sigma_S \approx 0.029$; for `G = 200`, $\sigma_S \approx 0.035$ (see [Measuring Strength When the First Player Wins](/docs/optimization/evaluation) for opening fairness and SPRT/sample-size limits).

Through iteration 12, equal-time matches used the 2.5x C4D worker inference ratio (640 searches for `v6b` against 1,600 for `v5`, 200 games). Once [`deploy/alphazero-oracle/README.md#L14-L17`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/deploy/alphazero-oracle/README.md#L14-L17) measured the Oracle A1's actual 2.5-second search counts (272 vs 634), the schedule switched to 270 vs 630 over 300 games from iteration 14 onward (and retroactively on iteration 13):

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Equal search counts | 1,600 vs 1,600 | Not recorded in config | Iter 12: **0.540** (`+28 Elo`) | `10x128` is stronger per simulation | [`v6b-10x128.yaml#L21-L22`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v6b-10x128.yaml#L21-L22) |
| Equal time (C4D ratio) | 640 vs 1,600 | 200 | Iters `4, 8, 12`: **`0.465, 0.475, 0.460`** | Flat below 0.500 at `lr = 0.0003` | [`v6b-10x128.yaml#L19-L21`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v6b-10x128.yaml#L19-L21) |

## The Search-Setting Sweep and Its Null Result

Because `v6b` had already reached `0.46–0.50` against `v5` at 270 vs 630 searches, one hypothesis was that search settings carried over from `v5`'s 800-search self-play regime — such as exploration constant `C` (2.0) or `fpu_reduction` (0.1) in [`MctsEngine::CalculateUcb`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/cpp/src/MctsEngine.cpp#L807-L828) (see [Monte Carlo Tree Search with PUCT](/docs/alphazero/mcts)) — might be suboptimal for a shallower 270-simulation tree:

$$
\text{UCB}(s, a) = Q(s, a) + c_{\text{puct}} \cdot P(s, a) \cdot \frac{\sqrt{N_{\text{parent}} + 1}}{1 + N_{\text{child}}}
$$

At 400 games per setting, the binomial standard error near even odds is $\sigma_S \approx \frac{1}{2\sqrt{400}} = 0.025$ (a 95% confidence half-width near `±0.049`). Across **six search settings** tested over **400 games each**, every result fell in the narrow band **0.49 to 0.505** ([issue #49](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/issues/49)) — a null result with no configuration separating from 0.500.

*Archive limit*: neither the repository configs nor issue #49 record which six parameter combinations or checkpoint iterations were evaluated or the individual score of each of the six runs; only the sample count (400 games each), number of settings (6), and aggregate range (`0.49–0.505`) are preserved.

## The Paused Distillation Attempt

If `10x128` evaluates individual positions more accurately (0.540 at equal searches) while `6x96` runs 2.33x more searches per move on two Neoverse-N1 cores, knowledge distillation offers an alternative route: generate high-search policy labels with the `10x128` teacher and train a `6x96` student to imitate them offline so the deployed server keeps `6x96`'s speed.

Commit [`0039c4d`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/commit/0039c4d) added [`distill.py`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/scripts/distill.py#L44-L172) and student config [`small-6x96.yaml`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/small-6x96.yaml). Two design choices appear in [`label_game`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/scripts/distill.py#L44-L79):

1. **Diverse openings without label noise**: `collect_labels` plays 0 to `random_plies` (6 by default) uniform random opening moves before recording positions, then runs `sims` (800 default) noise-free teacher searches (`time_limit_s: None`, deterministic `_most_visited_action` selection) so policy targets are clean MCTS visit proportions $\pi^{\text{teacher}}$.
2. **True game outcome $z$ for the value head**: As documented in `label_game`'s docstring, values are set to the eventual game result $z \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$ rather than the teacher's scalar value prediction, because the teacher's own value head saturates and copying it directly would transfer that bias to the student:

```python
# alphazero/gomoku/scripts/distill.py:44-51, 72-79
def label_game(
    teacher: AlphaZeroEngine, sims: int, opening_plies: int, rng: random.Random
) -> list[GameSample]:
    """Play one teacher game and label each searched position.

    Policies are noise-free visit shares. Values are the game result because the
    teacher's own value estimates saturate and would copy that bias into the student.
    """
    ...
    return [
        GameSample(
            state=position,
            policy_probs=policy,
            value=_outcome(winner, int(position.next_player)),
        )
        for position, policy in labelled
    ]
```

[`run_train`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/scripts/distill.py#L139-L172) holds out 10% of shuffled teacher positions for [`holdout_losses`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/gomoku/scripts/distill.py#L123-L136) and trains the `6x96` student (`AdamW`) with `AlphaZeroTrainer` against the same cross-entropy-plus-MSE objective using teacher visit shares $\pi^{\text{teacher}}$ and game results $z$:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}}(\theta) = -\sum_{a} \pi^{\text{teacher}}_{a}(s) \log p_{\theta}(a \mid s) + \bigl(\hat{v}_{\theta}(s) - z\bigr)^2
$$

After implementing `distill.py`, `small-6x96.yaml`, and unit tests ([`test_distill_and_eval.py`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/tests/scripts/test_distill_and_eval.py)), the distillation attempt was **paused** ([issue #49](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/issues/49)). No large-scale teacher-labelling corpus, student holdout loss curve, or student-versus-`v5` strength match exists in the repository, so whether distilling `10x128` into `6x96` yields a stronger CPU model than `v5` self-play was left untested.

## The Open Question: Where v6b Stopped at Iteration 32

Even though `v6b` never crossed 0.500 in production-budget matches, stopping it did not settle the ceiling of the `10x128` run. At `lr = 0.0003` (iterations 4, 8, and 12 at 640 vs 1,600), `v6b`'s score against `v5` stayed flat (`0.465, 0.475, 0.460`). Once the learning rate dropped to 0.0001 at iteration 15 and 0.00005 at iteration 25, both evaluation series recorded in [`v6b-10x128-cont2.yaml#L3-L7`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v6b-10x128-cont2.yaml#L3-L7) and [issue #49](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/issues/49) climbed into the mid-20s:

- **Against `v5` iteration 144 (270 vs 630, 300 games, 3 opening plies)**: 0.480 at iteration 13 (`lr = 0.0003`), then 0.460 (16), 0.480 (20), 0.497 (24), 0.490 (28), and 0.487 (32).
- **Against `v6b`'s own `seed_v5games.pt` (iteration 0)**: 45.0% (10), 50.0% (15), 51.3% (20), and 52.5% (25) — game counts for the seed-check matches are not stated in `v6b-10x128-cont2.yaml`.

Two facts leave the outcome genuinely open:

1. **Every step is inside sampling noise**: With $\sigma_S \approx 0.029$ for a 300-game match, the difference between 0.460, 0.497, 0.490, and 0.487 is smaller than one standard error; the series rules out `v6b` already being decisively stronger than `v5`, not whether it was still improving slowly.
2. **Stopped for time and budget, not convergence**: [`v6b-10x128-cont2.yaml`](https://github.com/sungyongcho/gomoku/blob/f416558/alphazero/configs/v6b-10x128-cont2.yaml#L8-L39) planned through iteration 50 with a final decay to 0.00002 across iterations `41–50`, but the training cluster was shut down at iteration 32 due to wall-clock time and cloud budget.

Whether continuing `v6b` to iteration 50, combining `v6b` with [tree reuse and pondering](/docs/optimization/tree-reuse-and-pondering), or finishing the paused `10x128 -> 6x96` distillation would have surpassed pruned `v5` (671 searches in 2.5 s) at equal thinking time remains unanswered in this repository.
